What this is, and what is exact

The hyperbolic metric was computed on the glued mesh of the genus-6 curve (discrete conformal equivalence, Bobenko–Pinkall–Springborn), the surface developed into the disk, and its Fuchsian group read off from the side pairings. The tile is the Dirichlet polygon of that group about a base point: faces with the same number and color are glued. Corners of the same color are one point of the surface.

Live coloring: each pixel's point is located in one of 26 conformal charts (centers spread over the surface so every point is within 1.2 of one), which store a3 and a5; a5 is polished by Newton's method on the curve, then the free critical point is iterated in single precision on the GPU. Very deep zooms reach the limits of single precision and of the chart data (a3 accurate to ~1e-5).

Up to discretization error the picture is conformal: hyperbolic components keep their shape when moved to the center. Checks (angle sums, area 20π, side pairings, mesh convergence, real ovals geodesic) are in REGISTRATION.md (E92-U).

Real ovals (cyan, yellow): the two real circles of Per6(0); they are closed geodesics of this metric (E92-U6), drawn here as exact geodesics. G6 (E91): each edge joins two polynomial centers by the shortest geodesic on the surface between them (dashed: the second-shortest, for capture pairs of multiplicity 2). This is a stand-in for the capture path, not the path through the shared capture component itself.

Violet edges with an arrow (G6, checkbox above): the edge joins f to f′ where the label of f′ (the even numerator k of k/(26 − 1)) has more factors of 2 than the label of f; the arrow points to f′, and the line is thicker the larger the difference. White edges: both labels have the same number of factors of 2.

White dots: polynomial centers (label k means angle k/63). Red: punctures (marked points here).
